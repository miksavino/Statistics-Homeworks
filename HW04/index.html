Exercise 1 - Welford's Recurrence Formulas

Knuth's Recurrence Formula

One of the biggest challenges in computing a measure of location (such as the mean) from online streaming data is the lack of prior knowledge regarding the total number of data points that will be processed. Hence, online algorithms are preferred since they work without requiring the entire dataset to be available upfront.
A classic example of an online algorithm is Knuth's recurrence formula.
To compute the arithmetic mean dynamically, we isolate the newest observation xk: $$\bar{x}_k = \frac{1}{k} \left( \sum_{i=1}^{k-1} x_i + x_k \right) = \bar{x}_{k-1} + \frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}$$ .
The new mean equals the previous mean plus an adjustment term: the difference (xk - x̄k-1) scaled down by the current sample size k.
From the new mean, it is possible to calculate the mean shift: $$\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1} = \frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}$$ .
Furthermore, the post-update residual is: $$(x_k - \bar{x}_k) = \frac{k - 1}{k}(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})$$ .

Welford's Recurrence Formulas

As with computing the mean from a data stream, calculating the variance also suffers from catastrophic cancellation: when working with a dataset of very large values and a relatively small variance, subtracting two nearly identical large numbers leads to a critical loss of significant digits, sometimes even resulting in an impossible negative variance.
To prevent this issue, Welford extended the approach introduced by Knuth, applying it to avoid catastrophic cancellation when tracking the sum of squared deviations from the mean M2,k: \(M_{2,k} = \sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - \bar{x}_k)^2\) .
Welford expresses M2,k dynamically by decomposing each deviation (xi - x̄k) using the previous mean x̄k-1: (xi - x̄k) = (xi - x̄k-1) - (x̄k - x̄k-1).
Squaring both sides and summing from i = 1 to k: \(M_{2,k} = \sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 - 2(\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})\sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) + \sum_{i=1}^k (\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2\) .
Since the deviations of the prior (k-1) elements around x̄k-1 sum to 0 ( \(\sum_{i=1}^{k-1} (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) = 0\) ), each term simplifies:

  • \(\sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 = M_{2,k-1} + (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2\)
  • \(\sum_{i=1}^k (x_i - \bar{x}_{k-1}) = x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}\)
  • \(\sum_{i=1}^k (\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2 = k(\bar{x}_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2\)

Combining these terms: M2,k = M2,k-1 + (xk - x̄k-1)2 - 2(x̄k - x̄k-1)(xk - x̄k-1) + k(x̄k - x̄k-1)2.
Substituting the Knuth's mean shift: \(-2\left(\frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}\right)(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}) + k\left(\frac{x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1}}{k}\right)^2 = -\frac{(x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2}{k}\) .
Hence: \(M_{2,k} = M_{2,k-1} + (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2\left(1 - \frac{1}{k}\right) = M_{2,k-1} + (x_k - \bar{x}_{k-1})^2\left(\frac{k - 1}{k}\right)\) .
Finally, applying Knuth's post-update residual identity yields the Welford's recurrence formula: M2,k = M2,k-1 + (xk - x̄k-1)(xk - x̄k).
For any sample size k ≥ 2, the variance is: \(\sigma_k^2 = \frac{M_{2,k}}{k - 1}\) .


Below the Javascript code implementing Welford's recurrence formula:
let mean = 0, M2k = 0;

for (let i = 1; i <= k; i++) {
const x = stream[i - 1];
const delta = x - mean;
mean += delta / i;
M2k += delta * (x - mean);
}

const variance = k > 1 ? M2k / (k - 1) : 0;

Comparative Insights

  • Empirical vs. Theoretical Locations: both the final statistics and the average trajectory closely match the theoretical values. When looking at the average score, the variance drops toward 0 as the number of steps increases, confirming the Law of Large Numbers. When using the scaled score, the variance stays constant and the final histogram takes the shape of a classic bell curve, verifying the Central Limit Theorem.
  • Online Algorithm's Stability: by updating the values step by step, Welford's recurrence formula avoids precision errors caused by subtracting very large numbers. This keeps the calculations accurate and prevents negative variance, even when running large simulations with many steps and attackers.

Importance for Inferencial Statistics

  • Estimating Risk: in the real world, defenders do not know the exact vulnerability level p of a system. Since the average score gets closer and closer to p as more tests are run, Welford's recurrence formula allows to estimate this risk continuously without needing to save massive log files.
  • Building Real-Time Warning Bounds: since the scaled score follows a standard bell curve, analysts can set simple statistical thresholds: \(\bar{x}_k \pm z_{\alpha/2} \frac{\sigma_k}{\sqrt{m}}\) .
    If an attack trajectory moves outside these limits, security teams know that something abnormal is happening (such as a zero-day exploit) rather than normal random noise.
  • Predicting Worst-Case Scenarios: calculating all possible breach combinations across many servers is practically impossible. Thanks to the Central Limit Theorem, defenders can use the standard normal distribution to quickly estimate the probability of extreme, high-impact security breaches.

How Mean and Variance Change with Network Size n

  • Total Score: both the mean and the variance grow linearly with n. The spread between different trajectories increases as √n, meaning that attackers drift further apart across larger networks.
  • Average Score: the expected values stays constant at the true system vulnerability level p (or 0 in the Rademacher model), while the variance drops toward 0 at a rate of 1/n. By the Law of Large Numbers, randomness cancels out and all trajectories converge to a stable line.
  • Scaled Score: the variance remains strictly constant, completely invariant to n. This allows trajectories to preserve their dispersion, forming the classic bell curve predicted the Central Limit Theorem.
